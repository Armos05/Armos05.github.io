What Changed With Learning

first 5 trials latest 5 trials

Train for about 10 trials to fill this in. As the fly learns, the dopamine burst at the sugar shrinks because the reward is no longer a surprise. Avoid-MBON output drops, the central-complex goal signal grows, and the fly turns less. The Kenyon cells themselves don't change. What changes is the strength of their synapses onto the output neurons.

How This Works
  • Train: the fly runs trial after trial. Sugar at the cupcake triggers dopamine, and the bars get shorter.
  • Test: learning is frozen for one run. Press Reset Brain, then Test, to compare with an untrained fly.
  • Smell: odor arrives in patchy puffs blown by a meandering wind, \(c = \sum_p \tfrac{m}{r_p^2}\, e^{-d_p^2/2r_p^2}\).
  • Odor on / off: while smelling, the fly runs upwind; once the odor is lost, it slows, casts and drifts back.
  • Real wiring: each model neuron carries every FlyWire synapse of its cell type and side, 631,412 in all; ORN-to-PN, PN-to-KC, PN-to-LH and KC-to-MBON weights come from them.
  • Spiking brain: 977 leaky integrate-and-fire neurons, \(\tau_m \dot v = v_0 - v + g\). The AL, LH, KCs, APL and MBONs fire only through real synapses; ORNs, DANs and the CX fire random spikes at model rates.
  • Value: \(V = \sum_k \mathrm{KC}_k\,(a_k w^{\text{ap}}_k - b_k w^{\text{av}}_k)\), with \(a_k, b_k\) each KC's real synapses onto approach and avoid MBONs.
  • Dopamine: the surprise \(\delta = r + \gamma V' - V\); \(\delta > 0\) weakens KC-to-avoid synapses, \(\delta < 0\) KC-to-approach, and the opposite side recovers.
  • Steering: the fly turns by \(\sin(\theta_{\text{goal}} - \theta)\) toward the remembered food vector plus the wind goal.
  • Gradient field: pink is the expected reward, and the arrows show where the fly would steer in the average plume.
  • Glass brain: each flash is a simulated spike, with lines to the neuron's 3 strongest real targets; bars show region rates in Hz.
  • Data check: the table under the readouts compares this run with published fly recordings, live.
  • Limits: steering and learning still run on the rate model, with spikes alongside; the CX and DNs follow model rates; synapse and claw gains are calibrated.
What Matches The Literature
  • Wiring: real FlyWire synapse counts and transmitter signs, summed per cell type. Real data, type-level (Dorkenwald 2024; Shiu 2024).
  • Spiking neurons use the whole-brain LIF constants; two gains are tuned. Partly calibrated (Shiu 2024).
  • ORN + AL fire on each puff, favour the same-side antenna and normalize strength. Consistent (Olsen 2010; Gaudry 2013).
  • Plume tracking: upwind and faster during odor, more turning after it is lost. Consistent (Álvarez-Salvado 2018; Matheson 2022).
  • After losing odor real flies drift back downwind; the model barely does. Mixed (Álvarez-Salvado 2018).
  • Kenyon cells fire sparsely (~7%) through real APL feedback, and almost never without odor. Consistent (Honegger 2011; Turner 2008).
  • Dopamine bursts at sugar, then shrinks about half with training. Prediction; unclear in flies (Liu 2012; Schultz 1997).
  • MBONs lose avoid-drive where reward was found, specifically, but less than flies (~30% vs 80%). Partly consistent (Hige 2015).
  • Central complex path-integrates and steers to the goal through PFL3. Consistent; KC vector memory is a hypothesis (Lyu 2022; Westeinde 2024).
  • DNs: DNa02 turning settles and DNp09 forward drive rises. Consistent (Rayshubskiy 2020; Bidaye 2020).
  • Diverges: real place memory lives in the central complex, not the mushroom body (Ofstad 2011).
