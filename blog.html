What is that distinct smell of freshly cut grass?

Read moreClose

When grass is cut, it ruptures plant cells, activating enzymes that release aromatic compounds called green leaf volatiles (GLVs).

This smell is part of the plant's defence. GLVs can deter herbivores, and also attract predators of those herbivores, such as predatory mites and wasps, for protection. GLVs also signal other plants to step up their defences.

So, plants have each other's backs.
